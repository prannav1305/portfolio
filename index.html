Karma Yoga Initiative

Turning Village Engagement into a Business Idea

Karma Yoga is Great Lakes' signature community immersion program, where student teams are sent to a nearby village to work on a real, assigned problem statement. My team was given the topic of veterinary healthcare — with the challenge of not just supporting animal health in the village, but developing a viable business idea around it.

We proposed making incense sticks and mosquito/insect repellents from cow dung — a low-cost, sustainable product line that ties directly into local livestock care and gives the community an additional income stream. I represented the team on the ground: speaking directly with villagers to collect data points, understand their existing practices around cattle care, and gauge their genuine interest and willingness to participate in the business idea.

  • Assigned focus area: maintaining veterinary healthcare in the village
  • Proposed venture: incense sticks & repellents manufactured from cow dung
  • My role: on-ground representative — village interviews, data collection, interest validation

A Live Debate on India's Talent Exodus

Debattle Ground was a formal debate hosted by the International Relations Committee (IRC) at Great Lakes Institute of Management, Chennai, bringing together two teams of debaters to argue a live policy question in front of a judging panel:

"Does the exodus of skilled professionals weaken India's Innovation Ecosystem, or strengthen it through global networks?"

The format demanded structured argumentation under time pressure and sharp rebuttal skills — a direct extension of the stakeholder communication and persuasion skills I lean on professionally.

With the team before taking the stage at Debattle Ground.
Team · For the Motion

Arguing For the Motion on India's Talent Exodus

I was part of the team arguing for the motion — building the case that global mobility of skilled Indian professionals strengthens, rather than weakens, the domestic innovation ecosystem through diaspora networks, capital flows, knowledge transfer, and return migration.

  • Event: Debattle Ground, organized by the IR Committee, Great Lakes Institute of Management, Chennai
  • Format: 1 debate · 2 teams · 10 debaters
  • My position: spoke for the motion

NCC — Discipline, Drill & the 'C' Certificate

My time with the NCC ran alongside my engineering degree and shaped my discipline and leadership just as much as the classroom did. I earned my 'B' Certificate in 2020 after 52 weeks of training, and followed it with my 'C' Certificate in 2021 — again after 52 weeks — the senior-most certification awarded at the collegiate level, typically reserved for cadets who have also taken on command responsibility.

That responsibility came with the rank of Sergeant in 2021, where I helped train and lead fellow cadets through drill, parade discipline, and camp routines — reinforcing the same habits of accountability and structured execution that later carried into my professional work.
